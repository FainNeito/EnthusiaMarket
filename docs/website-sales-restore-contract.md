# Sales feed restore contract — proposal, not implemented

The current producer exposes numeric sale IDs and checkpoints. The website consumer identifies its checkpoint by endpoint URL and persists sales and cursor together, but has no producer-generation fence. A database restore or fork can reuse IDs or leave the same high-water mark while changing the underlying records. A cursor-only comparison can therefore miss sales or encounter conflicting identities.

The following is a joint design target informed by website owner review, not a claim of deployed behavior or safe recovery. Market PR #6 remains draft and the public feed remains incomplete.

**Prototype requirement (REQ-407, event-driven).** WHEN an authenticated v2 consumer requests a page, Market SHALL require its expected external generation and exclusive numeric cursor, return original recording UUIDs as event identities, and reject missing or mismatched generations and legacy requests. The isolated producer prototype is untested and does not establish safe recovery. The external generation requires correct operator rotation, and checking it before and after a read cannot detect an ABA transition, a rotation after the last check, or an omitted rotation. A consumer must still fence the whole write transaction, and both systems need an explicit paused, fenced reconciliation procedure.

## Required behavior

1. A producer generation identifies one continuous archive lineage. It stays stable across ordinary restarts and is replaced with a new globally unique generation before a restored or forked archive is served. A value stored only inside the restored database cannot independently detect restoration; generation storage and rotation must be outside that restored state and must be included in the operator procedure.
2. Every page carries its generation, and the consumer requests subsequent pages with the generation it expects. Missing, changed, malformed or mixed generation, a cursor regression, and an unsupported legacy transition fail closed. They must not silently reset the cursor or publish complete coverage.
3. Sale identity is collision-safe across generations and replay is idempotent. An immutable globally unique original event identity must survive backups, or the transition must stop for manual reconciliation with coverage explicitly incomplete. Simply prefixing every replay with a new generation would double-count retained sales; equal payloads or hashes alone must not merge legitimately distinct sales. A reused numeric ID representing a different sale must never overwrite or silently merge with the old sale. The current website's globally unique `market_sales` IDs and conflict behavior require an explicit versioned migration covering ingestion, deduplication, aggregates and public projections.
4. The consumer commits each validated page, coverage state and generation-bound checkpoint atomically. A compare-and-set or equivalent fence must abort the **entire page transaction**, including sale inserts and coverage updates, when the generation or version is stale. A conditional checkpoint update that affects zero rows while still committing the other writes is insufficient. Test competing pollers, including an old worker finishing after a generation switch. Cursor monotonicity applies within a generation; transitions require an explicit reviewed reconciliation protocol.
5. Legacy producers and checkpoints require an explicit, documented unchanged-source compatibility policy. The legacy `nextCursor` fallback can contain nonnumeric characters while the explicit checkpoint path requires signed-64-bit numeric values; negotiate and migrate persisted legacy state without assuming those formats are interchangeable. Absence of a generation is not evidence that a restore has not happened. Partial coverage stays marked incomplete and cannot be promoted to official rankings merely because a poll succeeds.
6. Define what happens to sales already held by the website but absent from the restored producer. Never silently delete them, resurrect them, or assume they were never real; require a reviewed reconciliation decision and retain incomplete coverage until it is resolved.

## Restore procedure and evidence needed

The future operator procedure must pause and fence website synchronization, stop Market writers, take and verify separate producer and consumer backups, assign and verify a new generation outside the restored database, reconcile retained sale identity and coverage, perform a reviewed reset/replay, and verify both stores before resuming. A clean outbox inventory alone cannot prove these steps or database downgrade safety. This proposal does not authorize or execute a production restore.

Before release, jointly review the exact producer and consumer schemas and deployed versions. Tests must cover ordinary restarts; restores before and after the checkpoint; reused IDs, equal payloads of distinct sales and duplicate replay; retained versus removed history; stale in-flight workers and concurrent pollers; malformed or missing generations; nonnumeric legacy cursors, old schema and upgrade/rollback; terminal and BUY-only pages, empty pages and backlogs over four pages; crash/retry boundaries; backup and restore of generation-control state; privacy; and explicit incomplete coverage. A production-equivalent end-to-end rehearsal and independently verified rollback plan remain required.
